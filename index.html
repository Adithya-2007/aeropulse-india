NAQI & Telemetry Glossary

Central Pollution Control Board Standards & Metrics
Official CPCB NAQI 0–500 Scale 5 Tiers
Tier 1 · Good 0 – 50
Minimal impact. Air quality is considered pristine and satisfactory; atmospheric exposure poses virtually no health risk. Safe for all demographic groups and outdoor activities.
Tier 2 · Satisfactory 51 – 100
Minor breathing discomfort may develop in sensitive individuals with severe respiratory vulnerabilities, chronic asthma, or lung hypersensitivity. Clean for the general population.
Tier 3 · Moderate 101 – 200
Breathing discomfort to people with lung disease such as asthma, and discomfort to children and older adults upon prolonged outdoor physical exertion.
Tier 4 · Poor 201 – 300
Breathing discomfort to most people on prolonged exposure. Significant cardiovascular and pulmonary distress in people with preexisting heart or lung ailments.
Tier 5 · Very Poor & Critical 301 – 500+
Respiratory illness and severe physiological distress on prolonged exposure. Unified national critical danger tier (combining very poor and emergency conditions). High distress even in healthy adults; severe hazard for elderly, children, and heart/lung patients. Outdoor physical activity should be strictly avoided.

CPCB NAQI Standard (MoEFCC) NATIONAL STANDARD

Statutory 6-tier linear piecewise breakpoint formula formulated by the Central Pollution Control Board (Government of India). Standardizes 24-hour and 8-hour continuous monitoring into a unified index (0 to 500+).
